# The ladder and the rope

A fixture essay for the M6 pipeline. Synthetic on purpose: it exists so the
generative path can be exercised without touching Jordan's material.

People say a skill is a ladder. You climb rung by rung, and the rungs are
ordered for you in advance by someone who has already climbed.

I think a skill is closer to a rope. It holds because many weak strands cross
each other, and no strand runs the whole length.

The ladder picture fails on transfer. A rung only bears weight in one
direction, which is why ladder-trained skill collapses the moment the problem
turns sideways.

Watch anyone actually good at a thing and you see splicing, not climbing. They
join short strands from unrelated jobs until the join disappears.

The practical consequence: practice should braid, not stack. Two hours across
three problems beats six hours up one.

There is an obvious objection. Curricula exist because ropes cannot be
inspected, and a ladder at least shows you which rung is missing.

The objection lands. A braid hides its gaps until it is loaded, and by then
you are hanging from it.

So the honest picture keeps both: climb where the ground is mapped, braid
where it is not, and know which kind of ground you are on.
